Classification of Pyroclastic Rocks

Dr. Ahmad Redaa

19-10-2025

What Are Pyroclastic Rocks?

Definition:
> Rocks formed by the consolidation of pyroclastic materials, fragments ejected during explosive volcanic eruptions.

They include:
- Air-fall deposits (tephra)
- Pyroclastic flows and surges
- Reworked volcaniclastic sediments (if recognizable as pyroclastic origin)

Basis of Classification

Pyroclastic rocks are classified according to:

  1. Fragment size (e.g., ash, lapilli, blocks/bombs)
  2. Component type (juvenile, lithic, accidental)
  3. Fabric and texture (welded, massive, bedded)
  4. Degree of consolidation (loose tephra vs. solid rock)
  5. Depositional process (fall, flow, surge)
  6. Distance from Source (Proximal, Distal)

1. Size-Based (Descriptive) Classification

Fragment Size Term (Unconsolidated) Term (Consolidated Rock) Typical Rock
< 2 mm Ash Tuff tuff
2–64 mm Lapilli Lapillistone / Lapilli tuff Lapilli tuff
> 64 mm (angular) Blocks Block breccia Dome-collapse breccia
> 64 mm (rounded/molten) Bombs Agglomerate Volcanic agglomerate

Key:
Tephra = unconsolidated deposits
Tuff = consolidated equivalent

2. Composition-Based (Genetic) Classification

Type Description Examples
Juvenile Derived directly from magma (pumice, glass, scoria) Pumice tuff, vitric tuff
Lithic Fragments of older volcanic/country rocks Lithic tuff
Crystal Dominated by crystals from the magma Crystal tuff
Mixed Combination of above Vitric-lithic tuff, crystal-lithic tuff

3. Textural Classification (After Fisher & Schmincke, 1984)

Texture Description Example
Nonwelded Loose or weakly compacted Air-fall tuff
Welded Hot pyroclasts fused by heat Ignimbrite
Stratified Well-bedded, sorted Tephra fall
Massive Structureless, poorly sorted Pyroclastic flow

4. Genetic / Process-Based Classification

Eruption Process Deposit Type Rock Example
Air-fall Fallout tephra tuff, lapillistone
Pyroclastic flow Ground-hugging hot mixture Ignimbrite
Pyroclastic surge Dilute, turbulent current Base surge tuff
Phreatomagmatic Water–magma interaction Tuff ring, tuff cone deposits

IUGS (1989) Classification — Simplified

Source: Winter, J. (2014)

IUGS recommends a two-step approach:
1- Identify pyroclastic origin,
2- Classify by dominant particle size and composition.

Example rock names:
- Vitric tuff — glass-rich, fine-grained (<2 mm).
- Lapilli tuff — mixed ash–lapilli, moderately sorted, common in fall or flow deposits.
- Lithic lapillistone — dominated by lithic lapilli, often vent-collapse or phreatomagmatic in origin.
- Ignimbrite — welded to non-welded deposit from a pyroclastic density current (flow facies).
- Agglomerate — coarse, bomb-rich pyroclastic rock composed of rounded or fluidal juvenile clasts, formed near volcanic vents during Strombolian or Vulcanian eruptions.

5. Example Naming Scheme

Rock name = Composition + Dominant size term

Composition Size Example Rock Name
Vitric <2 mm Vitric tuff
Crystal-rich 2–64 mm Crystal lapilli tuff
Lithic-dominated >64 mm Lithic breccia
Mixed (juvenile + lithic) variable Lapilli tuff or ignimbrite

6. Ignimbrites (Special Case)

Definition:
> A deposit from a pyroclastic density current (hot, turbulent flow of pumice, ash, and gas).

Characteristics:
- Often welded or compacted (fiamme texture)
- Poorly sorted, massive units
- Contain pumice, ash, and lithic fragments
- Record collapse of eruption column (Plinian-type eruptions)
